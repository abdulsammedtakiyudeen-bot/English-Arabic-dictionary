FREE · Grammar

Build a simple nominal sentence

A basic Arabic nominal sentence often begins with a noun or pronoun. The first part is the topic (مُبْتَدَأ), and the information about it is the predicate (خَبَر).

الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ
Al-baytu kabīrun
The house is big.
الطَّالِبُ مُجْتَهِدٌ
Aṭ-ṭālibu mujtahidun
The student is hardworking.

Notice that Arabic often does not use a present-tense word for “is” in this sentence pattern.

FREE · Grammar foundation

1. The definite article: الـ (al-)

In Arabic, الـ is commonly placed at the beginning of a noun to make it definite, often corresponding to “the” in English.

ArabicTransliterationMeaning
كِتَابٌkitābuna book
الْكِتَابُal-kitābuthe book
بَيْتٌbaytuna house
الْبَيْتُal-baytuthe house

When الـ is added, the final tanwīn (the double vowel ending) is normally removed. The article is written as ال even when its pronunciation changes before certain letters.

Remember: The letters that affect the pronunciation of ل in الـ are traditionally grouped as “sun letters”; with “moon letters”, the ل is pronounced clearly. Learn the spelling first, then practise listening to the sound.
Quick check

Question: Which expression means “the house”?

Answer: الْبَيْتُ (al-baytu).

FREE · Grammar foundation

2. Masculine and feminine nouns

Arabic nouns have grammatical gender. Many feminine nouns end in ة (tāʾ marbūṭah), although this is a useful pattern rather than a rule that works for every noun.

ArabicTransliterationMeaning
طَالِبٌṭālibunmale student
طَالِبَةٌṭālibatunfemale student
مُعَلِّمٌmuʿallimunmale teacher
مُعَلِّمَةٌmuʿallimatunfemale teacher

Adjectives usually agree with the nouns they describe in gender and number. For example, طَالِبٌ مُجْتَهِدٌ means “a hardworking male student”, while طَالِبَةٌ مُجْتَهِدَةٌ means “a hardworking female student”.

Quick check

Question: What is a common feminine form of طَالِبٌ?

Answer: طَالِبَةٌ (ṭālibatun).

FREE · Grammar foundation

3. Singular, dual and plural

Arabic distinguishes one, two, and more than two. The dual is a special feature that English usually expresses with “two”. The exact endings depend on grammatical position and word type, so begin by recognising the pattern.

FormArabicMeaning
Singularطَالِبٌone student
Dualطَالِبَانِtwo students (nominative form)
Pluralطُلَّابٌstudents

Arabic plurals include sound plurals and broken plurals. A broken plural changes the internal pattern of the word, as in طَالِبٌ → طُلَّابٌ. It is often best to learn a noun together with its plural form.

Quick check

Question: Which form above means “two students”?

Answer: طَالِبَانِ (ṭālibāni).

FREE · Sentence building

6. The iḍāfah: showing possession or connection

An iḍāfah is a common Arabic noun construction that links two nouns. It can express possession, association or a relationship. The first noun is the مضاف (muḍāf), and the second is the مضاف إليه (muḍāf ilayh).

كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ
Kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi
The student's book.
بَابُ الْمَسْجِدِ
Bābu al-masjidi
The door of the mosque.
  • The first noun is normally not given ال or tanwīn in this construction.
  • The second noun is in the genitive case.
  • Read the two nouns together as one connected expression.
Quick check

Question: What does كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ mean?

Answer: The student's book.

FREE · Verb patterns

7. Past and present verbs

Arabic verbs change form to show who performs an action and when it happens. These examples introduce the idea using the verb “to write”.

ArabicTransliterationMeaning
كَتَبَkatabahe wrote
يَكْتُبُyaktubuhe writes / is writing
أَكْتُبُaktubuI write / am writing
نَكْتُبُnaktubuwe write / are writing

In many beginner examples, the present-tense form begins with one of the letters أ، ن، ي، ت. These are sometimes taught together as the present-tense prefixes. The full conjugation depends on the subject.

أَنَا أَكْتُبُ دَرْسًا
Anā aktubu darsan
I write a lesson.
Quick check

Question: Which form means “I write”?

Answer: أَكْتُبُ (aktubu).

FREE · Conversation practice

8. At the market: a short dialogue

الْبَائِعُ: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ
Al-bāʾiʿu: as-salāmu ʿalaykum
Seller: Peace be upon you.
الزَّبُونُ: وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ. بِكَمْ هَذَا؟
Az-zabūnu: wa ʿalaykumu s-salām. Bikam hādhā?
Customer: And peace be upon you. How much is this?
الْبَائِعُ: بِعَشَرَةِ دَرَاهِمَ
Al-bāʾiʿu: bi-ʿasharati darāhima
Seller: It is ten dirhams.
الزَّبُونُ: شُكْرًا
Az-zabūnu: shukran
Customer: Thank you.

This is a simple model dialogue. Currency names and prices differ by country; replace دَرَاهِمَ (dirhams) with the currency used in your own practice situation.

Quick check

Question: What does بِكَمْ هَذَا؟ ask?

Answer: “How much is this?”

FREE · Reading comprehension

10. Reading passage: A learner's day

Read the short passage once without looking at the translation. Then read it again and identify familiar verbs and nouns.

أَنَا طَالِبٌ. أَسْتَيْقِظُ صَبَاحًا. أَذْهَبُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ. أَقْرَأُ كِتَابًا وَأَكْتُبُ دَرْسًا. أُحِبُّ اللُّغَةَ الْعَرَبِيَّةَ.

Transliteration: Anā ṭālibun. Astayqiẓu ṣabāḥan. Adhhabu ilā al-madrasati. Aqraʾu kitāban wa-aktubu darsan. Uḥibbu al-lughata al-ʿarabiyyata.

Show English meaning

I am a student. I wake up in the morning. I go to school. I read a book and write a lesson. I love the Arabic language.

Comprehension questions and answers
  1. Who is speaking? A student.
  2. Where does the student go? To school.
  3. What does the student read? A book.
  4. Which language does the student love? Arabic.
FREE · Study skills

11. A simple seven-day revision routine

  • Day 1: Review 10 familiar words and learn 5 new words.
  • Day 2: Read the words aloud and write each one once.
  • Day 3: Build five short sentences using pronouns and verbs.
  • Day 4: Review question words and practise asking questions.
  • Day 5: Read the short passage and answer the comprehension questions.
  • Day 6: Practise the market dialogue with a study partner.
  • Day 7: Test yourself without looking at the notes, then revisit difficult items.

Short, regular practice is usually more useful than trying to memorise a very large amount in one sitting. Keep a notebook of words you confuse and review them again later.
